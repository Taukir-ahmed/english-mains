# English mains: accuracy through practice

Prepared on 14 October 2026 for IBPS Clerk/CSA and RRB Officer Scale I. Reading comprehension is excluded throughout this plan. Cloze tests, paragraph ordering, sentence insertion and odd-sentence exercises remain included because they are separate requested question formats.

## My recommendation

Build one offline HTML practice trainer that chooses your daily work, teaches through questions, and automatically brings back weak rules. Use your checklist as the source library behind it. You should not have to choose a PDF or read a checklist each day.

Your basics and previous prelims successes mean a full beginner grammar course is unnecessary. Your score of 14 and your report of many mistakes justify starting with a diagnosis of application, question selection and accuracy. They do **not** establish which of those caused the lost marks. You also explained that you usually study when the exam gets close. The plan therefore starts with a small repeatable session instead of depending on a sudden long study routine.

Confidence will come from three things: recognising the format, being able to justify the answer, and seeing the same skill work on unseen questions under time pressure.

## What deserves the most practice

### Revised focus: learnable skills with recurring scoring value

Following your clarification, begin with four core areas: selected grammar rules in error/correction questions; cloze and fillers; connectors; and contextual word usage/swaps. The first grammar targets are subject-verb agreement, modal + base verb, parallelism, pronouns and common preposition patterns. These have identifiable solving cues and support several question formats. Their ease for you still needs confirmation from fresh questions.

Cloze is a high-priority recurring family, but its presence, size and variant are not guaranteed. Do not finish a vocabulary syllabus before beginning it. Use a small word-and-collocation lesson alongside each learning set, then solve the set and collect only the words or expressions that caused difficulty. In assessment mode, use unseen sets without vocabulary previews or hints.

For the first 14 days, use roughly half of fresh practice for cloze/fillers, one quarter for selected grammar, and the remaining quarter for connectors and word usage. Due mistake reviews come first. Start ordinary word cloze before unsuitable-word, multiple-fit and idiom cloze. A useful session is four due reviews, one five-question cloze set and four short grammar/filler/connector questions, followed by explanation and a fresh correction example. Learn three to five useful words or expressions from that work, including their normal partners and one confusing alternative.

Keep para jumbles, complex multi-column matches and multi-error combinations for the second stage, after these core skills become reliable. Retain a small amount of exposure so these formats are not unfamiliar later. Spelling is a short maintenance task, not a main study block. After two weeks, use fresh mixed results to decide which deferred family is most worth adding.

### Grammar to learn through mistakes

Use short contrast questions, not chapters of theory. Cover subject-verb agreement with intervening phrases; each/every/either/neither; a number/the number; singular head nouns; tenses and time clues; modal + base verb; conditionals; inversion after negative starters; parallel structures; pronouns and reference; adjective/adverb forms; articles and determiners; countability and quantifiers; comparisons; prepositions and verb complements; infinitive/gerund choices; relative clauses; modifier attachment; conjunction pairs; redundancy; active/passive and reported-speech forms where needed.

Pay particular attention to **no sooner ... than**, **hardly/scarcely ... when**, **not only ... but also**, **both ... and**, **neither ... nor**, and combinations such as **despite + noun/-ing** versus **although + clause**. Do not convert these into rules that ignore context, accepted alternative usage, or the full sentence.

### Vocabulary should be usable, not merely familiar

Your existing file contains **72 sentences, 260 highlighted occurrences, and 88 distinct headwords**. The first section repeatedly uses a small vocabulary set; recognising it is different from choosing correctly between close options.

Retain it as a seed library. Convert each useful word into a meaning-in-context item, a collocation choice, and a fresh usage question. Learn contrasts such as alleviate/exacerbate, bolster/undermine, prudent/reckless, plausible/feasible and liquidity/solvency. Introduce new words from vetted practice failures before expanding to long arbitrary lists.

Repair source examples first. For instance, disseminating accurate information to “obfuscate malicious rumours” teaches the wrong intended relationship; **dispel rumours** is the clearer construction. “Deteriorate stakeholder trust” is awkward in this context; use **erode stakeholder trust** or **cause stakeholder trust to deteriorate**. “Bolster interest rates” should be rewritten with the intended action, such as **raise interest rates**. Do not copy awkward vocabulary examples into scored drills.

## Mistakes the trainer should specifically expose

These are traps visible in your materials or plausible errors to test for; I have no candidate-level data showing that everyone makes them.

| Trap | Correct solving habit |
|---|---|
| Answering “fits” when the question asks “does not fit” | Identify the requested direction before looking at options; include mixed-direction practice |
| Choosing the nearest noun as the subject | Find the head of the subject, then test its verb |
| Picking a familiar synonym | Test meaning, tone, grammar and natural collocation together |
| Checking only the first blank or first sentence | Require the same option to satisfy every blank/sentence |
| Finding one correct statement in a combination answer | Evaluate each statement independently, then select the combination |
| Swapping one pair and stopping | Re-read the complete sentence after every proposed swap |
| Reordering everything despite a fixed fragment | Lock the fixed position; solve around it |
| Guessing paragraph order from topic alone | Build pronoun/reference, cause-effect, chronology and example links |
| Changing a valid sentence because “there must be an error” | Include genuine no-error/no-change answers |
| Treating a disputed key as a personal failure | Flag and quarantine the item pending review |
| Increasing attempts before stabilising accuracy | Increase coverage gradually, using evidence from fresh timed sets |
| Remembering an old answer without understanding it | Test the same concept in a new sentence with new distractors |

## The daily learning-by-doing loop

The proposed normal session is **30-40 minutes**. It is a planning default, not a daily limit you supplied.

1. **5 minutes: retrieve.** Answer four due review items without notes.
2. **12-15 minutes: learn one weak pattern.** Answer eight fresh questions. Commit an answer and confidence level before seeing the explanation.
3. **8-10 minutes: transfer.** Answer four mixed questions with the chapter label hidden.
4. **5-10 minutes: repair.** Review wrong, skipped and low-confidence answers. Record one cause and one short solving cue; solve a new contrast item.

That is about **16 scored items**, not a demand to rush through a large quantity. A five-question cloze counts as five items but remains one intact set. Reviews can use whole sets when their context is necessary.

On a busy day, complete a **10-15 minute maintenance session**: four due reviews and four fresh questions with explanations. Do not compensate for a missed day with an exhausting double session; resume the queue. Once weekly, take a longer timed non-RC set and spend comparable time analysing it.

Use the loop: **attempt -> commit -> explain -> contrast -> delayed retry -> fresh transfer**. An immediate retry is a correction check, not evidence of mastery. Schedule later checks around 1, 3, 7, 14 and 28 days, adjusting the queue to the exam date and performance. A wrong high-confidence answer receives priority.

## Exam selection strategy to practise

Start with your own best-performing formats from the diagnostic; no fixed topic order works for everyone. Make a first pass through questions where you have a clear solving path. Mark difficult but solvable items for a second pass. Revisit only when you can use a rule, context clue or elimination argument.

As initial **practice** limits, try roughly 45-60 seconds for an isolated item and 3-4 minutes for a five-question cloze/ordering set. Adapt them to measured accuracy and the official section timing. A set needs more than the sum of individual guess limits because its shared context takes time to read.

In learning mode, show explanations immediately. In test mode, hide explanations, topic labels and teaching hints until submission. Record low-confidence correct answers too. Do not adopt an absolute “never guess” rule or blind last-minute attempts; determine how well your reasoned elimination performs in practice and consider its time cost.

## Specification for the single offline HTML trainer

The opening screen should have **Start today's practice**, **Review mistakes**, **Timed practice**, and **Progress**. The daily recommendation should be automatic; selecting chapters remains optional. This is what removes the daily checklist burden.

### Content and explanations

- A first release should contain the diagnostic and one vetted batch spanning every major family. Expand in checked batches towards roughly **600-800 distinct non-RC items**, including new variants; this is a proposed build scope, not an audited count of ready-to-use questions.
- Each question stores stable ID, family, micro-skill, difficulty, passage/set ID where applicable, exact directions, answer, explanation, trap, source file and PDF page/question reference.
- Distinguish **source question**, **corrected/adapted source question**, and **new question inspired by a format**. Never present an invented or repaired item as an unchanged PYQ.
- Each explanation identifies the decisive clue, supplies the corrected sentence/order, and explains the nearest tempting wrong answer. For combination questions it evaluates every statement; for fillers it checks every blank.
- Quality gates: complete context and options, correct highlights, defensible answer, no accidental second valid answer, coherent explanation, correct source attribution. Ambiguous or broken questions go to an unscored review area.

### Adaptive review and reliable progress

- Prioritise overdue mistakes, high-confidence errors and weak recurring skills; keep a portion of the queue for fresh mixed questions.
- Track accuracy, coverage, net score, time, confidence calibration and retention separately. Show familiar-item results separately from fresh tests.
- Use concept-level mastery: at least three correct fresh variants across separate sessions, including a delayed check, plus successful mixed transfer. Repeated clicks on one remembered item do not establish mastery.
- Store progress locally in the same browser, with a visible storage warning if saving fails. Provide JSON export/import and a backup reminder; local browser storage alone is not a guaranteed backup.
- No login or internet should be needed for the question bank. Use readable mobile/desktop layouts, keyboard access, adjustable text size and clear set navigation. Keep your original PDFs and vocabulary HTML intact.
