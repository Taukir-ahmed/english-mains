## Review decisions

- Contextual synonym choices preserve the actual sense; nearby concepts such as convincing/clear and practicable/believable are explained separately. Antonyms explicitly request an opposite and do not silently change direction.
- A stronger conclusion is not automatically a synonym: corroboration supports a claim; refutation disproves it; a plausible account is not necessarily conclusive.
- Pronoun placement is checked for separable rule it out/carry them out and inseparable look into it. Candidate orders vary so the combination key is not constant.
- Confusable drills distinguish the forms and constructions of affect/effect, principal/principle, complement/compliment, discreet/discrete, credible/credulous, adverse/averse and related pairs. Effect as a verb meaning bring about is correctly retained.
- The assurance drill uses person + that-clause to avoid treating regional ensure/insure overlap as an absolute error. The energy-efficiency item does not score economic as categorically impossible.
- Spelling items use unambiguous conventional forms; standard regional spellings are not introduced as false alternatives.
- Error counts specify one count per independently faulty phrase. Every inserted fault stores its correction and reason; clean paragraphs and genuine no-error cases are retained. Formal fewer + countable plural is used as the exam-style convention, not a statement about all colloquial usage.
- Hybrid questions require both the ordering and the lexical answer. Multi-word restoration checks all positions, including a three-cycle, a four-cycle and a genuine unchanged order. Dual corrections require both statements to be repaired.
- Each context is independently authored so feedback on one question does not expose a later answer from the same paragraph.
